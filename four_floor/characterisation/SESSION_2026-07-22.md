# Day 2 session record — 2026-07-22

Frequency-shift damage pilot. The decisive experiment: does damage produce a
measurable modal shift? All captures in `characterisation/day2_baseline/`,
`day2_damaged/`, `day2_repaired/`. Analysis by `freq_shift_detector.py
--match-by-order`.

## 2. Method

- Primary detector: **ringdown** (Day 1 resolution 0.14% on f1 vs 3.59% sweep).
- 5 taps per state (baseline / damaged / repaired), top-floor displace-release.
- Every tap analysed for **all three modes** (f1, f2, f3), not just f1.
- Amplifier untouched across all three states; shaker off throughout.
- Same-day baseline: Day 1→Day 2 drift is ~1.2%, so each session needs its own.

## 4. Results — modal shifts

Order-matched (each set's own modes; damage moved f1 far outside any fixed band).

| Mode | Baseline | Damaged | Shift | p | Repaired | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f1 | 2.909 ± 0.012 | 1.206 ± 0.010 | **−58.5%** | 1e-13 | 2.939 ± 0.010 | 102% |
| f2 | 8.035 ± 0.010 | 6.761 ± 0.076 | −15.9% | 2e-6 | 8.107 ± 0.034 | 106% |
| f3 | 12.152 ± 0.002 | 11.918 ± 0.064 | −1.9% | 1e-3 | 12.176 ± 0.029 | 110% |

**Monotonic f1 ≫ f2 ≫ f3.** This ordering is the physical signature of
**bottom-storey** damage — the fundamental has the largest drift at the base, so
losing base stiffness hits it hardest. A localisation fingerprint from one
sensor, which the project had written off.

**Damping (independent indicator).** zeta1 rose 5.67% → 6.36% (baseline →
damaged), the expected friction direction, but not significant (p = 0.30).
Corroborating, not decisive; the frequency evidence carries the result.

**Amplitude control (gate b).** Baseline peak 1944 mg, damaged 2823 mg — a 1.45×
ratio that cannot move a resonance 59%. The shift is modal, not amplitude.

---

## 5. Reversibility and the reassembly floor

Repaired f1 = 2.939 Hz, a **102% recovery** from the damaged 1.206 back to the
2.909 baseline (fractionally past it). Damage fully reverses.

The detector flags repaired-vs-baseline as "significant" at +1.0%, but this is
statistical, not physical:
- +1.0% residual against a −58.5% damage signal (recovery 98–102%);
- in the WRONG sense for persistent damage (repaired is stiffer, not softer);
- repaired (2.939) is nearer the Day 1 value (2.944) than this morning's
  baseline (2.909) — i.e. within the known day-to-day drift.

What the +1% actually measures is **reassembly repeatability**: torquing back to
"original" does not land on exactly the same stiffness. This sets a floor for the
campaign — see §6.

---

## 6. Consequences for the matrix

Two floors, and the LARGER governs the lightest attributable damage grade:

- measurement floor (tap scatter, 5+5): **~0.6% on f1**
- reassembly + day drift floor: **~1–2% on f1**

So a damage grade is only cleanly attributable if it shifts f1 by **more than
~1–2%**, not merely more than 0.6%. Severe damage gave 58%, so there is large
headroom; the matrix should sample grades down toward the 1–2% floor to find
where detectability actually fails.

f3 is the most PRECISE detector (0.02% scatter) but the LEAST sensitive to this
damage (−1.9%); f1 is the noisiest (0.42%) but by far the most sensitive
(−58.5%). Recording all three lets the matrix use whichever gives the best
shift-to-noise per damage location — and the three-mode pattern is the
localisation signal.

## 7. Anomalies / caveats

- **Baseline drifted 1.2% from Day 1** (2.909 vs 2.944 raw), with the y-axis DC
  offset flipping +0.009 → −0.015 g. Suggests the rig was disturbed/remounted
  between sessions. Handled by same-day baselining; noted as evidence that
  "undamaged" is not stable across days.
- **f1 tap scatter 0.42%** (vs Day 1's 0.14% from only 2 taps). f1 is heavily
  damped (~6%), rings ~10–14 cycles, so it is inherently the noisiest mode. Not a
  fault; the Day 1 figure was under-sampled.
- **FIFO overruns** persist (bit-banged bus, 1.31× headroom). 1–2 per capture,
  do not shift peak frequencies. Move the ADXL345 to hardware bus 1 before the
  matrix if PSD magnitudes are ever needed.
- **freq_shift_detector required a fix mid-session**: fixed Day-1 mode anchors
  missed the 59% f1 shift. Added `--match-by-order` (discover each set's own
  modes, compare by order). All results above use it. Committed 0eb3696.

## 8. Verified empirically vs assumed

**Verified this session:** severe damage produces a large, significant,
reversible f1 shift; the shift is frequency not amplitude; the f1≫f2≫f3 ordering;
reassembly repeatability ~1%; detection floor 0.6%.

**Assumed / not established:** that lighter damage scales monotonically toward the
floor (the matrix will test this); that the f1≫f2≫f3 ordering inverts for
top-storey damage (untested — would confirm localisation); that reassembly
repeatability is ~1% for storeys other than 1 (only storey 1 tested).
