## Tests

Three statistics run on the same nine captures. Each uses a different subset
of the data, so they are not alternative versions of one test.

**The statistic quoted in Section 4.1.5.** Welch's two-sample t-test
comparing the *extreme gains only*, 1v4 against 2v8, three replicates each:
t = -0.63, df = 3.21, p = 0.573. This is the test the body cites.
It uses six of the nine captures and discards the middle gain entirely.

**The same trend over all nine captures.** A linear regression of f1
on drive RMS across every capture gives a slope of -0.120 Hz per unit RMS,
standard error 0.183, t = -0.66 on 7 degrees of freedom, p = 0.533,
r = -0.24. This tests the same hypothesis as the first statistic, that f1
trends with drive, but uses all the data and gains four degrees of freedom by
doing so. It agrees: no trend.

**What the first two both miss.** A one-way analysis of variance across
the three gains gives F(2,6) = 8.24, p = 0.019. The group means differ
significantly, and neither test above can see it. The extreme-gain comparison
cannot, because the 1v4 and 2v8 means differ by only 0.028 Hz while the
discarded 2v2 mean sits 0.147 Hz above their midpoint: the omitted group is the
deviating one. The regression cannot, because the displacement is not ordered by
drive, so a straight line through it has near-zero slope.

Taken together: all three agree that f1 carries no monotone trend with drive,
and the third shows that the swept-sine estimate nonetheless varies between
groups by more than its within-group scatter. That is a property of the estimator
and not of the structure, since the drive level within a gain group is nearly
constant while f1 is not. Of the three, the statistic quoted in Section 4.1.5 is the weakest: it uses the
fewest captures and is the only one blind to the deviating group.


## Resolution

The pooled within-gain standard deviation is 0.0518 Hz on 6 degrees of freedom.
Against the lowest-gain mean of 2.8836 Hz that is 1.79% of f1, and twice
it is 3.59%, the figure quoted in Section 4.1.5 and in the assumptions table.
The derivation is therefore


--- LaTeX (equation*) ---
\begin{equation*}
  \text{resolution} \;=\; \frac{2\,s_{\text{pooled}}}{\bar{f}_{1,\text{lowest gain}}}
  \;=\; \frac{2 \times 0.0518}{2.8836} \;=\; 3.59\%,
\end{equation*}
--- end ---

a two-standard-deviation figure, normalised by the lowest-gain group mean.

Two standard deviations is a rule of thumb rather than a formal detection limit.
The minimum difference this design could detect in a two-sample comparison at
three replicates per group, α = 0.05 and 80% power, is 0.153 Hz, or
5.30% of f1.

The formal threshold is therefore *above* the two-sigma figure, so the test
is less sensitive than 3.59% suggests. This strengthens the argument the
figure is used to make. Section 4.3 invokes the resolution to establish that this
test cannot license the trace-grade shifts, which run from 0.97% to 2.29%;
a true threshold of 5.30% places those shifts further below it, not closer to
it. The statement in Section 4.1.5 that the design resolves only shifts above
roughly 3.6% therefore holds a fortiori.

## Conclusion

No dependence of f1 on drive amplitude was detected across a factor of 2.2 in
drive. This is an absence of a detected effect and not a demonstration of
linearity: the design resolves shifts above roughly 3.6% by a two-sigma
criterion, and above roughly 5.3% formally, so an amplitude dependence smaller
than that would not have been found. The graded-series conclusions of Chapter 4
do not rest on this test. Tap amplitude across the graded series spans a factor
of 2.5 by set mean, inside the range tested here, and the within-cell tap
scatter of Table [table] bounds the combined effect of drive
variation and estimator noise at below 0.3% at every trace cell.

APPENDIX A.3 — Why no permutation test of the location effect is valid
========================================================================
Section 4.4 states that no valid permutation test of the location effect exists
for this design. The arithmetic behind that statement follows. The failure is one of experimental
design, and the classification result stands on the effect size at the end of
this section.


## The two candidate permutation units

A permutation test needs a unit that is exchangeable under the null hypothesis.
Two candidates are available, and each fails for a different reason.

The *run-level* unit shuffles the location labels of individual severe runs.
The *group-level* unit keeps the three replicates of a location together and
shuffles only the four location names.

Both spaces are small enough to enumerate exactly, so no sampling is needed
anywhere in this section.


--- LaTeX (table) ---
\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Permutation spaces for the two fixed signature spaces. The run-level
count is the number of distinct labellings of the label multiset. The
partition-preserving count is the number of relabellings that map the partition
of runs into classes onto itself, which for a perfect classifier is exactly the
set of labellings reproducing the observed score.}
\label{tab:perm-spaces}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Signature space & Runs & Classes & Run-level space & Group-level space & Partition-preserving \\
\midrule
Two-mode $(f_1,f_3)$   & 11 & 4 & 92\,400 & 24 & 6 \\
Three-mode             &  9 & 3 &  1\,680 &  6 & 6 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
--- end ---

The run-level counts are multiset permutation counts. With class sizes
3,3,3,2 in the two-mode space and 3,3,3 in the three-mode space,


--- LaTeX (equation*) ---
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{11!}{3!\,3!\,3!\,2!} = 92\,400,
  \qquad
  \frac{9!}{3!\,3!\,3!} = 1\,680 .
\end{equation*}
--- end ---


## Exact run-level p-values

Both classifiers are perfect on the observed labelling, 11 of 11 and 9 of 9.
Because leave-one-out nearest-class-mean depends on the data only through the
partition of runs into classes, the labellings that reproduce a perfect score are
exactly those that preserve that partition, which are the relabellings permuting
equal-sized classes among themselves. There are 3!=6 of them in each space.
The exact p-values are therefore closed-form rather than estimated:


--- LaTeX (equation*) ---
\begin{equation*}
  p_{\text{two-mode}} = \frac{6}{92\,400} = 6.5\times10^{-5},
  \qquad
  p_{\text{three-mode}} = \frac{6}{1\,680} = 0.0036 .
\end{equation*}
--- end ---


## Why neither unit gives a usable test

**Run-level permutation is anticonservative.** The three replicates of a
cell share one teardown, one damage application and one rebuild. They are
therefore correlated even under the null hypothesis that location carries no
information, and they are not exchangeable with runs drawn from other cells.
Treating them as independent narrows the null distribution and makes the
resulting p-value smaller than it should be. A defect of this kind can only
turn a true null into a false positive, never the reverse.

**Group-level permutation has zero power by construction.** This is the
stronger statement and it does not depend on the data. The scorer is invariant
under the null's own relabelling operation: a group-level permutation permutes
class *names* while leaving the partition of runs into classes identical, so
every distance the classifier computes is unchanged and the score cannot move.
Enumerating both spaces exhaustively confirms it. All 24 group-level labellings
in the two-mode space score 11 of 11, and all 6 in the three-mode space score 9
of 9. The attainable p-value is 24/24 = 6/6 = 1.0000 in every case.

This is not a null result. A null result means an effect was tested for and not
found. Here the statistic cannot change whatever the data are, so it would
return the same value on a dataset with a real location effect and on one with
none.

**Consequently the design admits no valid test.** With three replicates
nested inside a single rebuild per cell, location cannot be separated from
rebuild. The only unit that is genuinely exchangeable under the null is the one
whose relabelling the statistic ignores.


## The design change that would fix it

Replicating the *rebuild* rather than the taps. Three independent teardowns
and rebuilds per location, each with its own damage application, would make the
rebuild the exchangeable unit and give a permutation test with real power against
the location effect. This is a change to the experiment, not to the analysis, and
it is recorded as future work in Section 5.


## The effect size reported instead

The separation of the class means carries the result. Both signature spaces are reported because they measure different things:
the two-mode space includes Floor 3, whose second mode is voided by a harmonic,
and the three-mode space does not.

## Rounding convention

The floors quoted throughout Chapter 4, 0.30, 0.46 and 0.32%, are twice

## What n = 5 supports

Five cycles give four degrees of freedom, so these floors are operational
thresholds rather than formal statistical limits. A 2σ interval estimated
from five observations carries wide uncertainty in its own right, and no
distributional claim is made about the reassembly process. The floors are used in
Chapter 4 as a measured yardstick for what a rebuild moves, which is what a
deployed node would face, and not as a confidence bound. Where a margin is close
to its floor, the conclusion is stated as provisional for that reason.
